# Chapter 2: Two-Person Non-Zero-Sum Games

## Core Idea
When players' interests are not strictly opposed, cooperation can yield better results than pure competition, but rational self-interest often leads to suboptimal outcomes (Nash Equilibria).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Nash Equilibrium**:
  - When to use: Analyzing non-cooperative games.
  - How: Identify cells in a payoff matrix where neither player can improve their payoff by changing only their own strategy.
- **Prisoner's Dilemma**:
  - When to use: When individual rationality leads to collective irrationality (e.g., arms races, price wars).

## Key Takeaways
1. Non-zero-sum games often require communication, trust, or enforcement mechanisms to reach optimal outcomes.
2. Threats and promises (strategic moves) can change the game's payoff structure to induce cooperation.
